device-independent quantum key distribution · step-by-step

E91 DIQKD Protocol Visualizer

A source emits entangled pairs; one photon flies to Alice, the other to Bob. Each of them independently picks one of three random measurement angles per round. Some angle-combinations generate a shared secret key; the rest are sacrificed to compute a CHSH Bell-inequality value S. Only if |S| clears the classical bound of 2 — impossible for any local hidden-variable model, however the devices were built or tampered with — do Alice and Bob trust the key. That's what "device-independent" means: security doesn't rely on trusting the internals of the measurement devices, only on the observed statistics.

Alice's angles  a₁=0° · a₂=45° · a₃=90°
Bob's angles  b₁=45° · b₂=90° · b₃=135°
■ key-generating pair (a₂,b₁) or (a₃,b₂)
■ CHSH test pair (a₁,b₁),(a₁,b₃),(a₃,b₁),(a₃,b₃)

The source produces the singlet state |ψ⁻⟩ = (|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2. Whenever Alice and Bob happen to choose the same physical angle (45° or 90°), quantum mechanics guarantees their results are perfectly anti-correlated — that pair becomes a raw key bit. The four mismatched combinations built from {a₁,a₃}×{b₁,b₃} are used only to estimate S = E(a₁,b₁) − E(a₁,b₃) + E(a₃,b₁) + E(a₃,b₃). A genuine singlet gives |S| = 2√2 ≈ 2.828 (Tsirelson's bound); any eavesdropping strategy consistent with local realism is capped at |S| ≤ 2 (Bell/CHSH bound) — no matter how cleverly Eve fakes the correlations.

Everything runs live in your browser via Math.random() — nothing is sent anywhere. With Eve on, the "source" secretly replaces each entangled pair with two ordinary polarized photons correlated only classically (a local hidden variable model) — the strongest attack Bell's theorem allows us to rule out without trusting the devices.

01 The source emits entangled pairs

Each round, the source produces a pair in the singlet state |ψ⁻⟩. Neither photon has a definite polarization on its own — only the correlation between them is fixed, until someone measures.

03 Alice and Bob each choose a random angle and measure

Every round, Alice independently picks one of a₁/a₂/a₃ and Bob independently picks one of b₁/b₂/b₃ — chosen after the photons are already in flight, so no signal could tell one side what the other picked.

04 Public announcement of angles & classification

Alice and Bob publicly announce only which angle they used each round — never their outcome. Rounds split three ways: matching angles become key bits, the four CHSH combinations become test rounds for the Bell inequality, and the rest are discarded.

05 CHSH Bell test & key extraction

The four test-round correlations combine into the CHSH value S. This is the device-independent security check: it doesn't matter what's inside Alice's or Bob's boxes, only whether the observed statistics could be explained by any local hidden-variable (classical) model.
