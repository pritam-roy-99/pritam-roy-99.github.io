B92 Protocol Visualizer

Alice encodes each bit in one of only two non-orthogonal states — no basis choice on her side at all. Bob randomly picks a measurement basis for each incoming qubit; most results are ambiguous and get thrown away, but whenever he gets a result that is impossible for one of the two states, he knows the bit with certainty. Toggle Eve on to see how intercepting non-orthogonal states necessarily introduces detectable disturbance.

bit 0 → |0⟩ (Z eigenstate, 0°)
bit 1 → |+⟩ (X eigenstate, 45°)
|1⟩ — Z-basis "flag" outcome
|−⟩ — X-basis "flag" outcome

Unlike BB84, Alice never chooses a basis — bit 0 and bit 1 are simply two fixed, non-orthogonal states. Bob measures in Z or X at random. A Z-basis result of |1⟩ is only possible if the incoming state was |+⟩ (bit 1); an X-basis result of |−⟩ is only possible if it was |0⟩ (bit 0). Getting the "trivial" outcome (|0⟩ in Z, or |+⟩ in X) tells Bob nothing, since either state could have produced it — those measurements are inconclusive and discarded.

Everything runs live in your browser via Math.random() — nothing is sent anywhere. Eve's toggle is for demonstration only: in reality Alice and Bob can't see it, they can only infer her presence from the error rate on the conclusive results.

01 Alice prepares her qubits

For every bit, Alice sends a fixed, pre-agreed state: |0⟩ for bit 0, |+⟩ for bit 1. There is no basis to choose — the security of B92 comes purely from the fact that |0⟩ and |+⟩ are non-orthogonal, so no measurement can perfectly distinguish them.

02 Photons travel down the quantum channel

Each photon travels from Alice to Bob. Watch what happens when Eve is present.

03 Bob measures each photon

Bob picks Z or X at random for each incoming photon and measures. If he gets the "flag" outcome (|1⟩ in Z, or |−⟩ in X) his result is conclusive and tells him the bit with certainty. Any other outcome is inconclusive.

04 Public announcement of conclusive results

Over an ordinary public channel, Bob announces only which positions gave him a conclusive result — never the results themselves, never his basis choices. Alice already knows what she sent for those positions, so both sides now share a sifted key. Everything else is discarded.

05 Estimating the error rate & extracting the key

Alice and Bob publicly reveal a random sample of their sifted bits to check for disturbance. A low error rate means the channel — and the rest of the sifted key — can be trusted.
