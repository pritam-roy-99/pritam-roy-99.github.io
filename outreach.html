Featured demonstration

Quantum eraser.

Polarisation first marks the alternatives, suppressing the ordinary interference term. An analyser then projects into a basis in which the relevant alternatives become indistinguishable within the selected subensemble.

Conceptual route

What changes when path information exists?

With no path record, amplitudes associated with the alternatives can interfere. If orthogonal polarisation states label the two paths, the alternatives become distinguishable and the usual interference pattern is suppressed.

After projection onto an analysing basis that does not preserve the original path labels, the selected subensemble can again show interference. The lesson is complementarity and available path information, not retrocausality.

Interference is not destroyed by “observation” as a vague concept. It is controlled by whether the relevant alternatives remain coherent and indistinguishable in the measurement context.
